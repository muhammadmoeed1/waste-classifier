# Paper Recycling

Paper is widely recyclable but degrades in fiber quality each time it's reprocessed,
so it can typically only be recycled 5-7 times before the fibers become too short.

## How to prepare it
- Keep paper dry and clean — wet, food-stained, or greasy paper cannot be recycled
  and should go in general waste or compost.
- Remove plastic windows from envelopes if possible.
- Shredded paper can usually be recycled but should be bagged separately (in a paper
  bag, not plastic) since loose shreds can jam sorting machinery — check local rules.
- Sticky notes, receipts (thermal paper), and laminated paper are generally NOT
  recyclable due to chemical coatings.

## What happens to it
Paper is pulped with water, cleaned of inks and contaminants, and reformed into new
paper products such as newsprint, tissue, or packaging.

## Common mistakes
- Recycling greasy or food-soiled paper (e.g. napkins, paper towels) — these belong
  in compost or general waste, not recycling.
- Assuming thermal receipt paper is recyclable — most receipts use BPA-coated
  thermal paper that contaminates the recycling stream.
