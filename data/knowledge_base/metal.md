# Metal Recycling

Metals — primarily aluminum and steel in household waste — are highly valuable to
recycle since they can be reused indefinitely and recycling them uses far less
energy than producing new metal from raw ore.

## How to prepare it
- Rinse cans and containers to remove food residue.
- Crush aluminum cans if possible to save space (check local guidelines — some
  facilities prefer cans uncrushed for their sorting equipment).
- Labels do not need to be removed; they are burned off or separated during processing.
- Small scrap metal (foil, bottle caps) can sometimes be balled together to prevent
  it from falling through sorting equipment — check local rules.

## What happens to it
Aluminum recycling uses about 95% less energy than producing new aluminum from bauxite ore.
Recycled metal is melted down and reformed into new cans, car parts, or construction materials.

## Common mistakes
- Including metal items contaminated with hazardous materials (e.g. aerosol cans
  with remaining pressurized contents, paint cans) — these need special disposal.
- Assuming all "metal-look" packaging is recyclable — some chip bags and pouches
  are actually multi-layer plastic/foil composites that are NOT recyclable curbside.
