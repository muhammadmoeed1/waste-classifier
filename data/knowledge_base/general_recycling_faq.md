# General Recycling FAQ

## Why does contamination matter so much?
A single contaminated item (e.g. a greasy pizza box, or a plastic bag) can force an
entire batch of recyclables to be rejected and sent to a landfill instead, because
sorting facilities process material in bulk and often can't isolate a small
contamination once it's mixed in.

## What does the recycling symbol (chasing arrows) actually mean?
The triangular "chasing arrows" symbol does not automatically mean an item is
recyclable in your area — for plastics, the number inside the triangle is a resin
identification code, not a guarantee of recyclability. Always check local guidelines.

## Does recycling actually get recycled?
It depends on local infrastructure, market demand for recycled material, and
contamination levels. Rinsing items, removing non-recyclable components, and
following your local program's specific rules significantly increases the chance
material is actually recycled rather than landfilled.

## What is "wishcycling"?
Wishcycling is putting an item in the recycling bin because you hope or assume it's
recyclable, without actually checking. It's one of the leading causes of contamination
in recycling streams and can do more harm than simply throwing the item in the trash.
