# Plastic Recycling

Plastic recycling is more complex than other materials because there are many types
of plastic (identified by resin codes #1-#7), and not all are accepted by every
recycling program.

## How to prepare it
- Rinse containers to remove food and liquid residue — contaminated plastic is one
  of the most common reasons entire batches of recyclables get rejected.
- Remove non-plastic parts where practical (e.g. pumps on lotion bottles).
- Check the resin code (the number inside the triangle) against your local program's
  accepted list — PET (#1) and HDPE (#2) are the most widely accepted.
- Flatten bottles if your local program requests it, to save space.

## What happens to it
Accepted plastics are sorted by resin type, shredded into flakes, melted, and
extruded into pellets used to manufacture new plastic products, synthetic fibers,
or construction materials.

## Common mistakes
- Recycling plastic bags, film, or wrap in curbside bins — these typically need to
  go to dedicated store drop-off bins because they jam sorting machinery.
- "Wishcycling" — putting non-recyclable plastic in the bin hoping it gets recycled,
  which can contaminate an entire load and cause it to be sent to a landfill.
- Recycling plastics with heavy food contamination (e.g. a takeout container full
  of sauce) without rinsing first.
