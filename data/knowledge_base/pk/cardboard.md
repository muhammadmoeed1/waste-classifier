# Cardboard Recycling in Pakistan

Most cities in Pakistan, including Karachi, do not have municipal kerbside recycling.
What exists instead is an informal scrap economy: a "kabaria" (door-to-door scrap
buyer) or a neighborhood "raddi" (waste paper) shop will buy clean cardboard by
weight. Cardboard is one of the more reliably resalable materials in this economy.

## What a kabaria will take
- Flattened cardboard boxes, packaging cartons, and cardboard cores (from tape rolls,
  fabric bolls, etc.) are almost always accepted.
- Larger quantities (e.g. from a shop or a house move) are worth calling a kabaria for
  directly rather than waiting for one to pass by — many operate by phone now.

## Approximate resale rate
Illustrative ballpark: roughly **8-15 PKR per kg** for clean, dry, flattened cardboard,
sold as part of the general "raddi" (waste paper) trade. This varies a lot by
dealer, city, and current paper-mill demand — treat it as a rough sense of scale, not
a quote.

## How to prepare it for resale
- Flatten boxes and keep them dry — wet or moldy cardboard is often refused outright
  or bought at a much lower rate.
- Remove tape, foam inserts, and plastic windows where practical; a little tape left
  on is usually tolerated, but heavy contamination lowers the price.
- Greasy cardboard (a pizza box, for example) is generally not wanted — the greasy
  section should go into general household waste, not the raddi pile.

## Common mistakes
- Assuming a kabaria pays a fixed, published rate — rates are negotiated and vary by
  dealer, volume, and city.
- Mixing wet or food-contaminated cardboard into a raddi pile, which can get the
  whole batch rejected or discounted.
