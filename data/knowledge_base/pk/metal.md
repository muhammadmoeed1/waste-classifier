# Metal Recycling in Pakistan

Metal is usually the single most valuable category to a kabaria or scrap dealer,
anywhere in the world — Pakistan's informal scrap economy is no exception. There
is a well-established, active local market for scrap metal.

## What a kabaria will take
- Aluminum cans (drink cans) are actively sought and are among the most valuable
  household items a kabaria will buy.
- Steel/tin food cans, old utensils, and other scrap steel are also accepted, though
  at a noticeably lower rate than aluminum.
- Larger scrap metal (old appliance parts, pipes, wiring) is typically handled by a
  dedicated "kabaria"/scrap yard rather than a household waste collector, and is
  often worth a special trip or phone call given its value.

## Approximate resale rate
Illustrative ballparks (these vary a lot by metal type, purity, and city):
- Aluminum cans: roughly **200-350 PKR per kg**.
- Steel/tin cans and mixed scrap steel: roughly **30-60 PKR per kg**.
These are rough, illustrative figures meant to convey that metal — aluminum in
particular — is by far the most lucrative household material to sell to a kabaria,
not a firm quote; always expect real-world offers to vary by dealer and by day.

## How to prepare it for resale
- Rinse cans to remove food/drink residue — a kabaria may discount visibly dirty
  cans, though this matters less here than for formal recycling programs.
- Separate aluminum cans from steel/tin cans if you can — mixing them means the
  whole batch may get weighed and paid at the lower steel rate.
- Crushing cans saves storage space but isn't required for resale.

## Common mistakes
- Assuming all "metal-look" packaging is real metal — some chip bags and pouches
  are multi-layer plastic/foil composites with no scrap value.
- Selling aerosol cans or paint cans with pressurized/hazardous contents still
  inside — these should be emptied/handled with care, not sold as-is.
