# General Recycling FAQ (Pakistan)

## What is a "kabaria"?
A kabaria (also spelled kabari/kabadiya) is an informal scrap/waste-material buyer —
often going door-to-door, or running a small scrap shop — who buys resalable
household materials by weight (metal, paper/cardboard "raddi", and some plastic)
and sells them on to recyclers, mills, or processors. This informal network is the
main "recycling" pathway in most Pakistani cities, since municipal kerbside
recycling programs like those in the US/UK/EU largely don't exist here.

## Are the resale rates in this app accurate?
No — they're rough, illustrative ballparks meant to give a sense of *roughly* what's
worth sorting out and roughly how the categories compare to each other (e.g. "metal
is worth much more per kg than paper"), not precise or authoritative pricing.
Real rates vary by dealer, city, volume, cleanliness of the material, and day-to-day
scrap-market conditions. Always treat a kabaria's actual quoted price as the real
number, not this app's estimate.

## Why doesn't glass have any value here, when it's "recyclable"?
"Recyclable in principle" and "has a local resale market" are different things.
Glass is technically recyclable, but it's heavy relative to its value and breaks
easily in transport, so most Karachi-area kabarias won't take it — there's no
buyer willing to pay for it, so in practice it goes to general waste.

## What's the single most valuable thing to set aside for a kabaria?
Metal, and aluminum cans specifically — they command by far the highest per-kg rate
of any common household material in this informal economy, well above paper,
cardboard, or plastic.
