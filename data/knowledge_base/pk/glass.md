# Glass Recycling in Pakistan

Glass is the material with the weakest local resale market of the standard
categories. Most kabarias will not take glass at all — it's heavy relative to its
value, breaks easily in transport, and there's little demand for it compared to
metal, paper, or PET plastic.

## What has (almost) no local resale path
- Most bottles, jars, and broken glass have essentially no buyer in the informal
  kabaria economy and should go into general household waste.
- Broken glass in particular should never be left loose in a waste pile — wrap it
  or place it in a container so waste handlers aren't cut.

## The one partial exception
- Some reusable glass bottles (certain returnable soft-drink or dairy bottles) are
  bought back directly by the shop or vendor that sold them, for a small deposit-like
  amount — this is a shop-return practice, not a kabaria/scrap transaction, and only
  applies to specific returnable bottle types, not glass in general.

## Approximate resale rate
Illustrative ballpark: **effectively 0 PKR per kg** for glass sold as scrap through a
kabaria. Any value that does exist (the shop-return case above) is per-bottle, not
by weight, and is specific to certain drink brands/bottle types.

## Common mistakes
- Assuming glass has similar resale value to metal or plastic simply because it's
  "recyclable" in the abstract — in Karachi's informal economy, in practice it
  usually doesn't.
- Leaving broken glass unwrapped in a general waste bag, which is a safety hazard
  for anyone handling it afterward.
