# General Waste / Non-Recyclable Trash

Items classified as "trash" are not accepted by standard curbside recycling and
should go in general waste, unless they qualify for a special disposal stream
(e.g. e-waste, batteries, or hazardous waste).

## Examples of common non-recyclable items
- Styrofoam / expanded polystyrene (most curbside programs do not accept it).
- Broken ceramics and drinking glasses (different melting point than container glass).
- Diapers, wipes, and other soiled hygiene products.
- Multi-material composite packaging (e.g. chip bags, some coffee pouches).
- Thermal receipt paper and heavily laminated paper.

## Special disposal streams (not general trash either)
- Batteries, electronics, and light bulbs often require dedicated e-waste drop-off
  points due to hazardous materials.
- Paint, chemicals, and motor oil require hazardous waste facilities.

## Why "when in doubt, find out" matters
Putting a non-recyclable item in the recycling bin ("wishcycling") can contaminate
an entire batch of genuinely recyclable material, sometimes causing the whole load
to be redirected to a landfill. When uncertain, check your local waste authority's
guidelines rather than guessing.
